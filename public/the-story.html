Where the name comes from

The Balboa Club

In the 1940s, on the corner of Newmark Avenue and Cape Arago Highway in Empire, Oregon — now part of Coos Bay — there was a club called the Balboa Club. It hosted live jazz, Dixieland and dance bands like the Elkhorn Express and the Wild Horse Theatre. It was owned by bartender Tony Contreras’s great-grandfather. per Bridgetown Bites, Nov 16, 2026, citing Coos County music historians

Send me opening newsSee the new room

“It burned down in the 80’s,” chef Sam Smith says. What survives is a matchbook and a couple of photographs of Tony’s great-grandfather — and an idea.

The team has been pulling at the thread ever since: Sam says he’s planning a trip to the coast, to the library in Coos Bay, to go through the old archives and find more about the club. The new restaurant isn’t a replica. It’s what the founders want to build on the name’s back.

“We wanted to be able to do it in spirit.”Sam Smith, chef — Bridgetown Bites, Nov 16, 2026

So the spirit translates forward: a room built around music and drinking, with a sound system made to be heard, dancing allowed, a pool table, and a kitchen running an old-school American menu — the way a good club’s kitchen did, in ’45. per Bridgetown Bites, Nov 16, 2026

Who is behind it

The team

Sam Smith — chef

The chef-owner of Tusk, and the force behind Yaowarat and OK Chicken & Khao Soi. Brought onto this project by his bartender from Tusk. per Bridgetown Bites

Tony Contreras — bar

The bartender at Tusk, now leading The Balboa’s cocktail and bar program. His family’s club is where the name comes from — and he’s the one who brought Sam in. per Bridgetown Bites

Really Cool Bars Group

Quinn Matthewstearn and David Hall — the crew behind Side Eye, Panther Club and Jackie’s. per Bridgetown Bites

ChefStable — central office

Kurt Huffman’s group — Grassa, Loyal Legion, St. Jack, Coopers Hall and more; Oregon Restaurateur of the Year 2019 — acts as central office for the project. per WhatNow Portland
